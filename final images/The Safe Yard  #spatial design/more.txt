
The basis of the conceptual solution is organic volumes that soften the rigid rhythm of the courtyard architecture. Soft, flowing forms of flower beds and lawns create a sense of enclosure and lightness, and let people explore the space intuitively and freely, without a rigid network of paths dictating movement.

Movement scenarios support both solitude and calm rest. Grasses and meadow flowers in the beds evoke natural meadows. The central lawn can also work as a lounging area.

Two parts of the courtyard converge at a meeting point: a cylindrical stele in mirror stainless steel acts as a focal object. Mirrored steel appears again in a stone-like object on the lawn at the heart of the main courtyard.

The primary ground surface is concrete—a contemporary finish that supports strong formal moves and conceptual gestures while keeping the composition clean. An important layer of the idea is the use of historic paving and stone in the landscape. Pairing modern paving with old cobblestone adds context tied to the site’s history. Grass strips are woven into the concrete, and gentle earthworks create a soft, undulating ground.

The pavilion refers to the historical layer too: it reads as a greenhouse or orangery volume, echoing the kitchen garden that once stood here. Its skin is a frosted material that admits light yet forms a sheltered interior, protected from casual views from outside.

Summer café terraces are not separated by fences or special paving—they belong to the garden. Trees and shrubs are planted directly on the terrace so the space feels alive.

The playground is shaped with earthworks. Alongside bright concrete-and-metal play structures, historic stones are integrated. Several pieces use recycled plastic.

Courtyard benches combine charred wood with metal end pieces. The wooden seats stay comfortable across seasons. Their long, regular silhouettes reinforce the landscape’s soft, fluid character.
